## In short

A network = neurons arranged in **layers**. Each layer computes a weighted sum then an activation, and passes the result on: that's [[propagation-avant]]. With [[couche-cachee|hidden layers]] **and** a [[non-linearite]], the network goes beyond a single line.

## Layers, width, depth

- **Input**: raw values, no computation. **Hidden**: makes its own cuts. **Output**: the answer.
- [[largeur-profondeur|Width]] = neurons per layer; **depth** = number of layers.
- [[parametres|Parameters]] of a layer ($n$ inputs, $m$ neurons): $m \times n$ weights + $m$ biases. Network 2 → 2 → 1: **9 parameters**.

## Why we need a non-linearity

Without an [[fonction-activation]], linear layers collapse into one:

$$
W_2(W_1x + b_1) + b_2 = W'x + b'
$$

You need **several layers AND a non-linearity**. The options are detailed in [[sigmoide]], [[tanh]], [[relu]].

The [[uat]]: one hidden layer with enough neurons can approximate any continuous function. But it says neither **how many** neurons nor **how to find** the weights → that's the job of [[descente-gradient]]. In practice we prefer **depth**: fold then cut, rather than cutting every hole.

## Pitfalls

- The input layer computes nothing.
- Stacking layers without activations is useless.
- $a^{(1)}$: (1) = layer number, not a power.
- The theorem says the network **exists**, not how to find it.

## Key takeaways

- Parameters: $m \times n + m$ per layer.
- XOR = OR − AND → 1 hidden layer is enough.
- Layers + non-linearity: both are needed.
- Forward pass: $z = Wx + b$, then activation, layer after layer.
