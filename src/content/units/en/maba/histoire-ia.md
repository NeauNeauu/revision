## In short

AI swings between two ideas: writing **rules** by hand ([[symbolisme|symbolism]]) or letting the machine **learn from data** ([[connexionnisme|connectionism]]). Today connectionism dominates, thanks to [[deep-learning]].

1. **Beginnings**: the [[turing]] asks "can a machine seem human?". The [[perceptron]] is the first machine that learns.
2. **First winter**: the perceptron can't do [[xor]] (not [[lineairement-separable]]). Disappointment → research slows ([[hiver-ia]]).
3. **Expert systems**: rule-based AI returns with [[systeme-expert|expert systems]]… too costly to maintain → second winter.
4. **Machine learning**: learning from data ([[machine-learning]]).
5. **Deep learning**: big data + GPUs + trainable deep networks → win at [[imagenet]], then the [[transformer]].

## Pitfalls

- Deep learning is a **subset** of machine learning, not its opposite.
- Expert systems **don't learn**: they are rules.
- Dates and names aren't the point: remember **how the ideas follow each other**.

## Key takeaways

- Symbolism = rules; connectionism = data.
- AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL.
- DL revolution = data + compute + deep networks.
- Perception → generative → agentic → physical.
