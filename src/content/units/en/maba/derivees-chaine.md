## In short

Instead of "nudging" each weight, we compute its slope **exactly** with a [[derivee|derivative]]. Through the network's steps, derivatives **multiply**: that's the [[regle-chaine]], the heart of [[retropropagation|backpropagation]].

## Where the cost comes from

Cost = **work for one slope** × **number of slopes**. Nudging = a full run, and only an estimate, repeated for every weight. Yet all weights share the same **last stretch** to the loss (output → $\hat y$ → $L$): we'd like to compute it only once.

As the nudge shrinks to 0, the average slope becomes the **exact slope**. For $f(w) = w^2$ at $w = 3$: $f'(3) = 6$.

| formula | derivative | example |
|---|---|---|
| constant | 0 | $36 \to 0$ |
| $w^n$ | $n w^{n-1}$ | $w^2 \to 2w$ |
| $a \cdot f(w)$ | $a \cdot f'(w)$ | $5w^2 \to 10w$ |
| sum | sum of derivatives | $4w^2 - 24w + 36 \to 8w - 24$ |

## Partial derivative

A [[derivee-partielle]] changes **one** variable, the others held fixed. For $f = x^2 y$: $\partial f / \partial x = 2xy$, $\partial f / \partial y = x^2$. For a weighted sum $z = w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b$:

$$
\frac{\partial z}{\partial w_i} = x_i \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial x_i} = w_i \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial b} = 1
$$

$$
\frac{\partial c}{\partial a} = \frac{\partial c}{\partial b} \times \frac{\partial b}{\partial a}
$$

Each step multiplies the incoming change by its own derivative. Useful formulas:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat{y}} = 2(\hat{y} - y) \qquad \sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)\,(1 - \sigma(z)) \qquad \text{ReLU}'(z) \in \{0, 1\}
$$

This product of slopes explains the [[gradient-disparait]]: factors < 1 multiplied over many layers give almost 0.

Network from the [[propagation-avant]] example ($h_2 = 3.5$, $\hat y \approx 0.68$, $y = 1$):

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial v_2} = \underbrace{-0.64}_{\partial L/\partial \hat y} \times \underbrace{0.22}_{\partial \hat y / \partial z} \times \underbrace{3.5}_{\partial z / \partial v_2} \approx -0.49
$$

Same as nudging ($-0.48$), but exact and **without re-running the network**: everything comes from the forward pass.

## Towards backpropagation

[[retropropagation|Backpropagation]] applies the chain rule **from the loss back to the input**, reusing the shared stretch: all slopes in **one backward pass**.

## Pitfalls

- $\partial z / \partial w_i$ = the **input** $x_i$, not the weight.
- $2(\hat y - y)$: prediction − label (the sign matters).
- $\sigma'$ is computed from the output: $\hat y (1 - \hat y)$.
- Each factor is evaluated **at the current values**.

## Key takeaways

- Derivative = exact slope; ∂ = one variable at a time.
- Chain rule: multiply each step's derivative.
- $\partial L / \partial v_2$ = (loss) × (sigmoid) × (value on the arrow).
- Backpropagation = chain rule in one backward pass.
