## In short

An [[fonction-activation]] is applied after the weighted sum. It brings the [[non-linearite]] without which layers collapse. To learn, it needs a **useful slope**: that's what separates the four functions.

## Why the slope matters

[[descente-gradient]] moves each weight according to its [[pente|slope]]. If the activation is flat, the slope is ~0 and the weight **stops moving**.

- Step: slope 0 everywhere → no learning.
- Sigmoid/tanh: slope ≈ 0 at the edges → very slow learning.
- ReLU: slope 1 on the positive side → the signal flows. But a neuron that's always negative is "dead".

## Zero-centring

With sigmoid all outputs are positive: the next weights tend to move **in the same direction**, hence a zig-zag path. Tanh, centred on 0, avoids this.

During [[retropropagation|backpropagation]], the error signal travels back layer by layer and is **multiplied by each activation's slope** (see [[regle-chaine]]). With slopes < 1 (sigmoid) it shrinks at each layer: the [[gradient-disparait]]. Early layers barely learn.

Historical fixes: train **layer by layer** (2006), then better **initialization**, better **activations** (ReLU) and better **training techniques**.

## Pitfalls

- Sigmoid and tanh **both** saturate; only tanh is centred.
- ReLU doesn't saturate on the positive side, but can die on the negative side.
- The vanishing gradient hits the **early** layers, not the last ones.

## Key takeaways

- Step: slope 0; sigmoid: (0, 1), saturates; tanh: (−1, 1), centred, saturates; ReLU: slope 1, dying ReLU.
- Flat curve → slope ≈ 0 → weight stuck.
- Over many layers: vanishing gradient.
