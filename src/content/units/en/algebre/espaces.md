## In short

A **subspace** is closed under addition and scalar multiplication. Two key subspaces of $A$: the **column space** $C(A)$ and the **null space** $N(A)$. A **basis** = independent vectors that span the space; their number = the **dimension**. The 4 subspaces of $A$ have dimensions $r,\ r,\ n-r,\ m-r$.

## 1. Spaces and subspaces

- Examples: $\mathbb R^n$; the $2\times2$ matrices ($M$); functions $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ ($F$); polynomials of degree $\le n$ ($P$); $Z=\{\mathbf 0\}$.
- Required: $\mathbf u+\mathbf v$ and $c\mathbf v$ stay in the set (+ the usual rules: commutativity, associativity, $\mathbf 0$, opposite, distributivity).
- **Subspace**: contains $\mathbf 0$ **and** $\mathbf v,\mathbf w\in S\Rightarrow \mathbf v+\mathbf w\in S,\ c\mathbf v\in S$ (⇔ all linear combinations stay inside).
- Subspaces of $\mathbb R^3$: **$\{\mathbf 0\}$, lines through $\mathbf 0$, planes through $\mathbf 0$, $\mathbb R^3$**.

## 4. Computing $N(A)$ and the rank

1. **Eliminate**: $A\to U$ (echelon), then $R=\text{rref}(A)$ (pivots = 1, zeros above **and** below).
2. Columns with a pivot = **pivot variables**; without = **free variables**.
3. For **each free variable**: set it to 1, other free ones to 0, solve → **special solution**.
4. $N(A)$ = all combinations of the special solutions.

## 5. Complete solution of $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$

If $A\mathbf x_1=\mathbf b$ and $A\mathbf x_2=\mathbf b$ then $A(\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2)=\mathbf 0$: two solutions differ by an element of the nullspace.

1. Reduce $[A\ \mathbf b]\to[R\ \mathbf d]$.
2. **Particular solution $\mathbf x_p$**: free variables $=0$, read the pivots from $\mathbf d$.
3. **Nullspace** $N(A)$: special solutions.
4. $\mathbf x=\mathbf x_p+\mathbf x_n$, $\mathbf x_n\in N(A)$.

E.g. $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&3&0&2\\0&0&1&4\\1&3&1&6\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf b=(1,6,7)$ → $\mathbf x_p=(1,0,6,0)$ and
$\mathbf x=\mathbf x_p+x_2(-3,1,0,0)+x_4(-2,0,-4,1)$.

**Solvability**: $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has a solution ⇔ the last $m-r$ equations of $[R\ \mathbf d]$ reduce to $0=0$. E.g. $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&2\\-2&-3\end{bmatrix}$ → need $b_1+b_2+b_3=0$.

| Rank | Shape of $A$ | $R$ | # solutions |
|---|---|---|---|
| $r=m=n$ | square, invertible | $I$ | **1** |
| $r=m<n$ | short and wide | $[I\ F]$ | **∞** |
| $r=n<m$ | tall and thin | $\begin{bmatrix}I\\0\end{bmatrix}$ | **0 or 1** |
| $r<m$, $r<n$ | not full rank | $\begin{bmatrix}I&F\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ | **0 or ∞** |

- **Full column rank** ($r=n$): no free variable, $N(A)=\{\mathbf 0\}$, at most one solution, independent columns.
- **Full row rank** ($r=m$): $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ solvable for **every** $\mathbf b$, $C(A)=\mathbb R^m$.

## 6. Independence

**Independent** columns: $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ only for $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ (they fill the whole space). **Dependent**: a non-trivial combination gives $\mathbf 0$ (they stay in a plane/line).

| $n\times n$ $A$ invertible | $A$ not invertible |
|---|---|
| independent columns | dependent columns |
| $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$: **one** solution | **0 or infinitely many** solutions |

E.g. the “cyclic” matrix $C=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1\\-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\end{bmatrix}$ has its 3 columns in one plane (they sum to $\mathbf 0$) → no inverse.

## 7. Independence, span, basis, dimension

- **Independent**: $x_1\mathbf v_1+\dots+x_n\mathbf v_n=\mathbf 0$ only if all $x_i=0$ (⇔ full column rank). A $\mathbf 0$ in the family ⇒ dependent.
- **Span**: their combinations fill the space.
- **Basis** = independent **+** spanning. Then every vector has a **unique** expression in the basis.
- $n$ vectors form a basis of $\mathbb R^n$ ⇔ they are the columns of an **invertible** $n\times n$ matrix.
- **More than $m$ vectors in $\mathbb R^m$ ⇒ dependent.**

- **Dimension** = number of vectors in a basis (same for all bases).
- **Pivot** columns of $A$ = basis of $C(A)$; pivot rows (of $A$ or $R$) = basis of the row space $C(A^T)$.

**Fundamental theorem (part I)**: $\dim C(A)=\dim C(A^T)=r$, $\ \dim N(A)=n-r$, $\ \dim N(A^T)=m-r$.
So $\dim C(A^T)+\dim N(A)=n$ and $\dim C(A)+\dim N(A^T)=m$.

E.g. $R=\begin{bmatrix}1&3&5&0&7\\0&0&0&1&2\\0&0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$ ($m=3$, $n=5$, $r=2$) → dims $2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 1$; basis of $N(R^T)$: $(0,0,1)$.

## Pitfalls

- A subspace must contain $\mathbf 0$: a **shifted** line/plane is not one.
- $C(A)\subset\mathbb R^m$ but $N(A)\subset\mathbb R^n$.
- $C(A)\neq C(R)$ in general (columns change under elimination); but $N(A)=N(R)$.
- Free variable set to 1, the **other** free ones to 0 (not all to 1).
- A basis of $C(A)$ = pivot columns **of $A$** (not of $R$: $C(A)\neq C(R)$). But $C(A^T)=C(R^T)$ and $N(A)=N(R)$.
- Particular solution: free $=0$; don't forget to add $N(A)$.
- Independent **and** spanning: either alone is not enough.
- $\dim N(A)=n-r$ (columns), $\dim N(A^T)=m-r$ (rows).

## Key takeaways

- Subspace = contains $\mathbf 0$ + closed under combinations.
- $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ solvable ⇔ $\mathbf b\in C(A)$.
- $N(A)$ = combinations of the $n-r$ special solutions; rank $r$ = number of pivots.
- $\mathbf x=\mathbf x_p+\mathbf x_n$; solvable ⇔ $\mathbf b\in C(A)$.
- Basis = independent + spanning; $\dim$ = size of a basis.
- 4 subspaces: dims $r,\ r,\ n-r,\ m-r$; $n$ vectors of $\mathbb R^n$ = basis ⇔ invertible matrix.
- Independent columns ⇔ $A$ invertible.
